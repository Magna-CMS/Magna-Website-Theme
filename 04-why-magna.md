## The CMS Problem

**Section heading:**
## Two decades is a long time in software.

The first great CMS platforms — WordPress chief among them — solved a real problem brilliantly. They put publishing in the hands of everyone, and in doing so they shaped the modern web. That contribution deserves respect, not revisionism.

But longevity has a cost. Decades of evolution also mean decades of accumulated technical decisions, backwards compatibility obligations and layered complexity. Every early choice — sensible at the time — becomes something later versions must carry.

None of this makes older platforms bad. It makes them products of their era. The question worth asking is different:

> If we were designing a CMS today — for today's websites, teams and threats — what would we choose?

Magna CMS is our answer.

## Why We Built Magna

**Section heading:**
## What modern teams actually need.

We built Magna because the people building today's web kept needing the same things:

**Flexibility** — projects that can change shape without changing platform.
**Contemporary architecture** — foundations that match how software is engineered now.
**Real extensibility** — a plugin system designed as architecture, not as an afterthought.
**Better control** — over the codebase, the extensions, and the direction of a project.
**API capability** — because websites now live inside ecosystems of other software.
**Sane development workflows** — version control, environments and deployment practices that developers already use everywhere else.

No single item on that list is exotic. What's rare is a CMS designed around all of them from the start.

## Built for Today

### Modern websites
Today's sites are fast, interactive and connected. They authenticate users, integrate services and behave like products. Magna treats that as the baseline, not the edge case.

### Modern teams
Marketers publish, designers systematise, developers extend — often in the same week, on the same project. Magna gives each of them tools that don't get in the others' way.

### Modern development
Framework conventions, APIs, versioned code, repeatable deployments. Extending Magna feels like normal software engineering, because it is.

### Modern business requirements
Security posture that stands up to scrutiny. Maintainability that survives staff changes. A platform that grows with the business instead of capping it.

## A Different Approach

**Section heading:**
## Four principles we won't trade away.

### Focused core
The core does content management — thoroughly, dependably — and resists scope creep. A smaller, well-understood core is easier to secure, maintain and trust.

### Expand when needed
Functionality arrives through extensions, chosen deliberately. Your platform reflects your requirements, not a superset of everyone's.

### Build your own
When no extension fits, developers create their own — with first-class extension points, not fragile workarounds. Custom functionality is a supported path, not a hack.

### No forced direction
Magna doesn't assume you're building a blog, a shop or anything else. Whatever the project becomes, the platform doesn't stand in the way.
