## Brand Pillars

### 1. Designed Today

**Explanation:** Magna is not an iteration on a twenty-year-old codebase. It is what a CMS looks like when its architecture, security posture and workflows are chosen now.
**Supporting messaging:** contemporary framework foundations; no legacy compatibility tax; security considered at design time, not patched in afterwards.

### 2. Focused Core, Open Edges

**Explanation:** The core stays lean and dependable. Everything else — commerce, membership, industry features — arrives through plugins and modules, added only when needed.
**Supporting messaging:** install what you need, nothing you don't; extensions can't destabilise a core they don't touch; build your own when the catalogue doesn't fit.

### 3. Developer-Grade

**Explanation:** Magna treats developers as first-class users. Clean architecture, APIs, and extension points that feel like working with a framework, not around a product.
**Supporting messaging:** build plugins, not workarounds; integrate with the tools you already run; version-control-friendly workflows.

### 4. Usable by Everyone Who Ships Content

**Explanation:** Architecture means nothing if the marketing team can't publish a page. Visual building, reusable components and clear workflows keep non-technical teams independent.
**Supporting messaging:** visual page builder; reusable sections, headers and footers; media and publishing workflows that don't require a developer on call.

### 5. Grows Past the Website

**Explanation:** A Magna project can start as a company site and become a portal, a directory, a membership platform or a custom application — on the same foundation.
**Supporting messaging:** start with a CMS, build whatever comes next; no forced business model; one platform across the project's whole life.

## Brand Voice Guidelines

**Sound like:** a senior engineer who also runs the company. Confident, precise, plain-spoken, slightly bold. Comfortable with silence — no claim is padded.

**Do:**

- Make one claim per sentence and let it stand.
- Translate every technical statement into a business outcome within a sentence or two.
- Acknowledge WordPress respectfully, at most once per page, then move on.
- Label capability honestly: Available / In development / Planned.
- Use British English.

**Don't:**

- Use "revolutionary", "game-changing", "next-level", "cutting-edge", "unparalleled", "seamless", "the future is here", "best CMS ever".
- Claim measured superiority ("faster than", "more secure than") without evidence.
- Say "modern" more than once per screen. Substitutes: designed today, contemporary, current, built for now, this decade's.
- Attack competitors, name-drop WordPress gratuitously, or imply its users chose badly.
- Write paragraphs longer than three sentences on marketing pages.
