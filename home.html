The Shift

Websites changed. Most platforms didn't.

WordPress changed how the world built websites, and it deserves that legacy. But the internet it was created for in 2003 is not the internet we build for today.

Websites are now applications. They talk to payment systems, CRMs and internal tools. They serve logged-in customers, not just readers. Performance is measured in milliseconds, security is a board-level concern, and development teams work with frameworks, APIs and version control, not FTP.

Most established platforms have spent twenty years adapting to that shift. Magna CMS was designed inside it.

This is what a CMS looks like when you design it now.

Sites became software

Integrations, authentication and application behaviour are now the baseline, not the edge case.

Expectations rose

Performance and security stopped being nice-to-haves and became requirements teams are accountable for.

Workflows matured

Frameworks, APIs and version control became normal engineering practice. A CMS should fit them.

Architecture

Architecture you can build a business on

Magna is built on a contemporary, framework-based foundation with a modular core, clear extension points and API access. Plugins add functionality without modifying core code, so upgrades stay predictable and customisations stay yours.

Commercially, that means customisation that doesn't turn into technical debt, a platform your next developer can pick up quickly, and a site that's still maintainable in year five instead of a rebuild waiting to happen.

Good architecture isn't a developer luxury.

FAQ

Answers to common questions

What is Magna CMS?

Magna CMS is an open-source, extensible content management system built on a contemporary framework. It pairs visual tools for content teams with a plugin architecture designed for developers. It's a foundation for websites and the applications they grow into.

Is Magna headless?

Magna is headless-first: content is served over an API, so any frontend can consume it: a JavaScript framework, a mobile app, multiple channels at once. But headless is a choice, not a constraint: switch on the ready-made website frontend with one click when you just want a site, and move between the two approaches without re-platforming.

Is Magna trying to replace WordPress?

No. WordPress shaped the web and remains a strong choice for many projects. Magna takes a different approach: it was designed in this decade, around today's architecture, security expectations and workflows. The right platform depends on the project.

Who is Magna for?

Developers who want a CMS that behaves like the software they already build, agencies standardising on a reusable stack, businesses whose projects may outgrow "website", and content teams who want to publish without a developer on call.

Can I build my own plugins?

Yes. That's the point of the architecture. Plugins are the supported way to add functionality, with first-class extension points. Custom development is a normal path, not a workaround, and your code stays upgrade-safe and version-controlled.
